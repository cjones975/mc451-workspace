**Reading:** <Chapter 3: Knowing and Knowing Well>
**Date:** <2026-09-11>

## Reflection
Ethics and boundaries within and regarding data collecting, testing of new media features 
and studies may be something necessary for a study to be completed or a number to be 
collected, but I've never thought much about how those boundaries truly exist, 
as they are all typically vague and convaluded for the sake of keeping internet users and 
study participants in willful ignorance. So, no, ethics are not are formality. It is proven 
when we are told to "opt-out" rather than to "opt-in" for new features and studies were are 
first forced to participate in before we must agree or decline. I have seen this firsthand 
in my own media usage, finding out through an informational TikTok that a new Facebook 
update has granted their AI model permission to train with whatever I post. As we touched 
on in class, if we were first given the option to "opt-in," nobody would do it. It's as 
simple as that. Making people aware of the eyes on them and certain aspects of their lives
and media tendencies changes their passivity to agreed to muddled terms & conditions or
random app updates/installations. It forces me to see media companies and study developers & 
researchers in a bit of a different light--as it starts to seem as though the boundary isn't
really there--and if it is at all, it's vague and messy, and the risk of the effect of 
consumers and audiences doesn't seem to play into the bigger picture. It makes me wonder:
who really is doing these data collections and research studies in a truly ethical way? Is it
even possible to be ethical about something that, just in is existence of needing information
to produce a result, is invasive? Much to think about.

# Week 04 Journal: <Repetition, Gaps, and Never-Ending Findings>



**Reading:** <Chapter 4: Intelligence Gathering>
**Date:** <2026-09-18>

  Prior to this chapter, I had never heard of the conversation metaphor. Of course
listening to prior context and being as knowledgable as possible in a conversation or a
debate is important, but putting it into this perspective--a room full of bustling
conversations and differing opinions, it helps to contextualize why listening and reading
and being as informed as possible (in conversation, in studying, in reading literature
reviews and writing them) is so imperative. It helps to avoid repetition, provide the utmost
context, and continue an ongoing dialogue (as mentioned in the chapter). One thing I do
wonder though, is how to avoid too much repetition when there are already so many sources
available to us? And how do we avoid that repetition when we must cite these sources, quote
them, reevaluate them to make or break our own points? 
  Of course, we must expand upon existing facts and data, but how do we do that without 
convoluting the information and making it too redundant? Is there ever a point where there is
nothing more to expand on and no more to add to the preexisting open and ongoing dialogue? I 
guess that's where identifying the gaps comes into play, but is it possible to have no gaps 
at all? Can a study built on years of prior research and conversation be without flaws or 
gaps--completely whole in its data and findings? I don't think that's possible, yet it is hard
not to wonder if at some point there is simply nothing more to gain or add to a 
study/topic/idea. Too many questions to ask with no true concrete answers--but these readings 
always make me want to question things the more than likely don't yet have an answer.

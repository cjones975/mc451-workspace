# Week NN Journal: <Paradigms, Passivity, Politics, and other P words.>

**Reading:** <Chapter 5: Theory as a Lens>
**Date:** <2026-09-25>

The three paradigms seem to be imperative when looking at your theories through a lens and
truly getting to the heart of them. I wonder if there is one that is often deemed more common 
or "correct" to assume? The way one would approach their studies and come to their conclusions
would differ vastly dependant on the paradigm that aligns with their philosphical commitments.
The theories listed are super interesting, and some I have never heard of before. I think the
"uses and gratifications" theory is super interesting, because while audiences are not passive
when it comes to searching for, curating, and consuming content that they are interested in or
believe will fill a specific need, I feel that a lot of audiences online have become passive 
in their media consumption. I find this most prevalent in times of turmoil and uprising, (in
the world, in politics, in local communities). When the content is not there to simply provide
entertainment, people become passive in not only how they consume what they watch, but in how
they feel towards it. A lot of political content, content regarding Palestine, large wildfires
and environmental disasters, etc, is met with little empathy, and the passivity also makes
audiences more susceptible to misinformation and ignorance, as they are more unwilling to fact
check the things they so quickly believe from a 30 second clip. A bit of a tangent there, but
as that theory was born in 1973, I feel that times have changed and I don't agree that
audiences are very active, but I do agree that ones own interest in content is what drives
them away from passivity.

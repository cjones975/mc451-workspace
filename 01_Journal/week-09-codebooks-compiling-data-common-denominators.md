**Reading:** <Chapter 9: The Rulebook and First Workspace>
**Date:** <2026-10-20>

The data is, to me, some of the scariest stuff in this process. Not how to find it, record it.
But more specifically how to integrate it into coding software and make a proper codebook to 
everything I might need in this process. In talking with you after class, it seems that two
codebooks will better break apart information based on TikTok information and Spotify
information, eliminating any redundancies that might occur in a singular codebook containing
all parts. I'm learning that not only making things less complicated for oneself is the way to
go, but ensuring that I'm not making the data and codebook so messy to look at is also going 
to make the process a lot better for myself. So, the plan is to have one codebook based around
all of my TikTok data will include: videos, date posted, song used, and how the song is used,
the next codebook will contain all of my Spotify data, including: songs from top 10 charts for
4 month scope, their rank on the chart, streaming numbers. The common denominator lies in the
songs, and will connect the two codebooks. The question I now have is this; how many channels
does that require? Do I make a separate ones for the song itself and the streams it receives?
Or does that mesh together as a channel? I'm leaning towards having each as an individual.
But do I risk having too many channels and overloading myself? Is that even possible? It could
be, or I could be entirely overthinking it. Staring at the data. it's hard not to let the 
worries of messing up consume me!
